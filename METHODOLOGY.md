# Methodology

The Virelix Transparency Project documents publicly available or lawfully obtained information about public technology and surveillance systems. The project is independent and does not speak for any agency, manufacturer, or operator.

## Evidence standards

- Every factual submission must include a legitimate source citation.
- Sources may include public contracts, procurement records, agency policies, public meeting materials, official announcements, court records, or reputable reporting.
- A record should identify its source title, publisher, publication date, URL, and source type when available.
- Uncertain information must be identified as uncertain rather than presented as fact.
- Missing information is shown as “Not publicly documented”; it is never inferred or manufactured.

## Verification classifications

- **Confirmed**: supported by strong, direct public evidence.
- **Public-record confirmed**: supported by an identifiable public record.
- **Community reported**: reported by a community member and awaiting stronger confirmation.
- **Unverified**: retained as a lead or development fixture, not established as fact.

## Location precision

- **Exact publicly documented**: a public source provides a specific location.
- **Approximate**: public information supports an area but not an exact point.
- **Deployment confirmed / exact location unknown**: deployment is documented without a publishable exact location.

Map coverage displays are informational only. They are not measurements, detection guarantees, blind-spot indicators, safe zones, or routes intended to evade law enforcement.

Corrections are encouraged. Maintainers may update, qualify, or remove records when sources change or a factual error is identified.
